# Sentence Observatory

Paste any text — each sentence becomes a star in a constellation, sized by length, colored by type.

## The Idea

Text becomes a night sky. Every sentence is a star: larger stars are longer sentences, brighter
ones are structurally complex, green stars are questions, golden stars are exclamations. Stars are
connected by faint constellation lines in the order they appear in the text.

Hover any star to read its full sentence, word count, and type. The result is a spatial map of
how a piece of writing moves — you can see a writer's rhythm at a glance.

## How to Run

Open `index.html` in any browser. No install required.

1. Click **sample** to see first lines of famous novels rendered as a constellation
2. Or paste your own text into the input area and click **Observe**
3. Hover any star to read its sentence

## Reading the Sky

| Star | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Large white | Long sentence |
| Small white | Short sentence |
| Bright white | High average word length (complex vocabulary) |
| Green | Question sentence |
| Gold | Exclamation |
| Connecting lines | Narrative order (read left to right along the arc) |

## Why It's Interesting

Writers think about sentences but rarely see them as a system. The Observatory reveals patterns
invisible when reading linearly: a cluster of short stars = staccato rhythm, one enormous star amid
small ones = a deliberate long sentence, a trail of green = an essay built on questions.

It's a diagnostic tool for editing, a visualization for teaching, and simply beautiful to look at.

## Orbioom Feeling

Dark observatory stage (deep ink background, not black) with a generative background star field.
The glass input panel slides up from the bottom. Stars animate in with staggered easeOut curves.
Live green for questions, rare gold for exclamations. The star glow uses radial gradients — the same
light vocabulary as the orb. Calm, spatial, considered.
