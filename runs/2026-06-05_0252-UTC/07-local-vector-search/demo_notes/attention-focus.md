# Attention and Deep Focus

Attention is the fundamental resource of mental work. Every notification, context switch, and
interruption is a tax on attention. The question is not how to work faster, but how to protect
the hours of genuine focus.

## Deep Work vs Shallow Work

Cal Newport's distinction: **deep work** is cognitively demanding activity performed in uninterrupted
concentration. **Shallow work** is logistical tasks that can be done while distracted.

Most knowledge workers spend 80% of their time on shallow work and call themselves busy.
Deep work is increasingly valuable and increasingly rare.

## Flow State

Csikszentmihalyi's flow: a state of complete absorption where time passes unnoticed and performance
peaks. Flow requires a task that is slightly harder than current skill — the sweet spot between
anxiety (too hard) and boredom (too easy).

Interruptions destroy flow. It takes 23 minutes to return to a task after a distraction (Gloria Mark).

## Practical Approaches

- Single-tasking over multitasking (cognitive switching cost is real)
- Time-blocking: defending calendar slots for deep work
- Monotasking environments: remove notifications, close tabs
- Regular digital detox: strengthening attention like a muscle

The goal is not productivity in the narrow sense but the ability to do your best thinking.
