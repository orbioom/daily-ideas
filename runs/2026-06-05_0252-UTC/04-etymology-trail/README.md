# Etymology Trail

Trace any word back through time — language family by language family — as an animated visual timeline.

## The Idea

Enter a word and watch its ancestry materialize as a vertical trail of glass cards, each node showing
the ancestor word, its language, its era, and a human note about how meaning shifted. The path from
"salary" (Latin: salt allowance for soldiers) to your paycheck is five minutes of reading history.

Every word carries centuries of culture. Etymology Trail makes that visible, card by card.

## How to Run

Open `index.html` in any browser. No install required, no API keys.

Type a word and press **Trace** or hit Enter. Click any suggestion chip to explore the dataset.

**Included words (15):**
`salary`, `window`, `muscle`, `disaster`, `calculate`, `panic`, `hazard`, `trivial`,
`serendipity`, `clue`, `paradise`, `robot`, `nice`, `assassin`, `algorithm`

## Why It's Interesting

Etymology is one of the most effective ways to learn vocabulary deeply — you understand *why* a word
means what it means. But most etymology tools are dry dictionaries. This formats the trail as a
designed experience: staggered card animation, language chips, era labels, and human-written notes
about the meaning shifts.

Extending the dataset requires only adding an entry to the `ETYMOLOGIES` object in the HTML.

## Orbioom Feeling

Glass cards materialize with a staggered entrance animation. The trail line uses the live green
accent fading from #86C79A to transparent — a literal thread through time. Language chips use
the green border/fill. The origin node (the modern word) has a solid green dot; ancestors are hollow.
Quiet, scholarly, calm.
