Method & references

Model. The Lotka–Volterra predator–prey system, with state x = prey and y = predator:

dx/dt = αx − βxy  ·  dy/dt = δxy − γy

Optional logistic-prey extension with carrying capacity K: dx/dt = αx(1 − x/K) − βxy.

Integrator, conserved quantity & citations

Integrator. Classical 4th-order Runge–Kutta (RK4), implemented directly (no library). Each step of size h evaluates the vector field f four times:

k₁ = f(s), k₂ = f(s + h/2·k₁), k₃ = f(s + h/2·k₂), k₄ = f(s + h·k₃), sₙ₊₁ = s + h/6·(k₁ + 2k₂ + 2k₃ + k₄).

Conserved quantity (classic model). V = δx − γ ln x + βy − α ln y is invariant along exact trajectories. We track its drift as an integration-quality check (undefined / skipped when a population reaches 0). It is not conserved under the logistic extension.

  • Lotka, A. J. (1925). Elements of Physical Biology. Williams & Wilkins.
  • Volterra, V. (1926). “Fluctuations in the abundance of a species considered mathematically.” Nature, 118, 558–560.
  • Runge–Kutta classical 4th-order method (Runge 1895; Kutta 1901).

Honest simplifications. This is a continuous, deterministic, well-mixed model: no demographic stochasticity, no spatial structure, no age classes, instantaneous interaction. Populations are dimensionless unless you assign units. The classic model's closed orbits are structurally fragile — small numerical error slowly drifts V; the logistic extension instead spirals to the coexistence equilibrium.
