Method & references

What is happening here?

k-means partitions points into k groups, each represented by a centroid (its mean). It alternates two steps until centroids stop moving:

  1. Assignment: attach each point to its nearest centroid (Euclidean distance).
  2. Update: move each centroid to the mean of its assigned points.

This is Lloyd's algorithm. Inertia (within-cluster sum of squares, WCSS) is the total squared distance from points to their centroid — it only decreases. k-means++ seeds the initial centroids far apart so the result is better and more stable. The silhouette score (−1…1) rates how well each point fits its cluster versus the nearest other cluster. The elbow plots inertia vs k; the "bend" suggests a good k.

References:

  • Lloyd, S. P. (1982). "Least squares quantization in PCM." IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 28(2):129–137. (Work from 1957.)
  • Arthur, D. & Vassilvitskii, S. (2007). "k-means++: The Advantages of Careful Seeding." SODA 1027–1035.
  • Rousseeuw, P. J. (1987). "Silhouettes: a graphical aid to the interpretation and validation of cluster analysis." J. Comput. Appl. Math. 20:53–65.
