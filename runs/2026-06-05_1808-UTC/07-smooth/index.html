Coefficient Sanity Checks

Mathematical invariants verified in the browser: smoothing coefficients must sum to 1; derivative coefficients must sum to 0; SG with p≥2 must reproduce a quadratic exactly.

Method & References

Algorithm: Savitzky–Golay least-squares polynomial convolution. For each sample point, a polynomial of degree p is fit (by ordinary least squares) to the surrounding window of 2m+1 points. The smoothed value is the polynomial evaluated at the window centre. For the d-th derivative, the d-th derivative of the polynomial is evaluated instead, scaled by d!.

w = A (AᵀA)⁻¹ eₓ₀
where A[j,k] = (j−m)ᵏ, eₓ₀[k] = x₀ᵏ (d=0) or k!/(k−d)! · x₀^(k−d) (d>0)

Implementation: The (p+1)×(p+1) normal-equations system (AᵀA)·v = eₓ₀ is solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting (coded from scratch in js/savgol.js — no linear-algebra library). Coefficients are computed once per unique evaluation offset; interior points share a single cached centred coefficient vector.

Constraints enforced: window must be odd and ≥ p+2; derivative order ≤ polynomial order; window ≤ signal length.

Edge policy: Asymmetric coefficients (Savitzky & Golay 1964, Appendix). The window is anchored at the signal boundary; the polynomial is evaluated at the offset of the target point within the window. This is exact — not an approximation — because the polynomial model does not change.

Primary reference:

Savitzky, A.; Golay, M.J.E.

Honest simplifications: Spacing between samples is assumed uniform (unit spacing, Δx = 1). Derivative values are in units of [signal unit / sample index]. For physical derivative values, divide by the physical sample spacing Δx.
